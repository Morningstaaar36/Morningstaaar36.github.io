Science claim checker

Science claim checker on GitHub: Can a GPU fact-check a science claim before you blink?

  • 5,183 papers searched in under 0.1 ms
  • Every quote verified

CUDA, C++17, Llama 3.2, ONNX Runtime, RAG

What it is

You type a scientific claim, for example "bariatric surgery has a positive impact on mental health". The program answers Supported, Contradicted or Not enough info, gives a confidence percentage, and explains its answer in plain English.

How it works

A small neural network turns the claim into 384 numbers that capture its meaning. Custom CUDA code, written from scratch, compares those numbers with 5,183 research abstracts on the GPU in under a tenth of a millisecond. A second model re-ranks the best candidates, Llama 3.2 reads them and gives the verdict, and every quote in the explanation is automatically checked against the paper it came from.

GridWise

GridWise on GitHub: Can a plain-English note change tomorrow's power plan?

  • Live on Azure
  • Checks its own schedule

Python, FastAPI, LLMs, SciPy, Docker, Azure

What it is

A campus operator has a day-ahead forecast of demand, solar power and electricity prices, plus free-text notes like "the rooftop solar panels will be washed from noon to 2 PM". Some notes change the plan and some, like a moved registration deadline, do not. GridWise works out which is which and returns the cheapest valid 24-hour schedule for grid, solar and battery.

How it works

A language model reads each note and turns it into one of six kinds of rule, such as reduced solar output, no battery charging in a time window, or a minimum battery reserve. A solver then finds the cheapest schedule that obeys every rule, and the service checks its own answer before replying. It runs in a Docker container on Azure and anyone can call it.

Molecules on a quantum computer

Molecules on a quantum computer on GitHub: Can a quantum computer get a molecule's energy right?

  • 692 runs checked against exact answers
  • One run on real IBM hardware

Python, VQE, CUDA-Q, IBM Quantum

What it is

A molecule's energy changes as its atoms stretch, bend or twist, and chemists need those curves. This project computes them for eleven molecules, including hydrogen, water and ammonia, with the Variational Quantum Eigensolver.

How it works

Across 23 scans and 692 optimisations, every result is compared with the exact answer, and the quantum result never dropped below it, as theory requires. Full curves are then rebuilt from just a handful of points with polynomial and spline fitting, and one hydrogen curve was run on a real IBM quantum processor.

SACA-Tox

SACA-Tox on GitHub: Is this molecule toxic? Ask it from two angles.

  • AUROC 0.84 to 0.99 on six tests
  • Graph + text

PyTorch, graph neural network, ChemBERTa

What it is

It predicts whether a molecule is toxic on six standard tests, including mutagenicity, heart toxicity, eye corrosion and biodegradability.

How it works

A graph neural network reads the molecule as atoms and bonds, a frozen ChemBERTa language model reads its text description (a SMILES string), and a gated fusion block combines the two views. On held-out data the AUROC score, where 1.0 is perfect, ranges from 0.84 to 0.99.

SFUGAG

SFUGAG on GitHub: Can a model stay fair on data it has no labels for?

  • No source data, no labels
  • Under review at AISTATS

PyTorch, PyTorch Geometric

What it is

A graph model trained on one network often becomes unfair when it is moved to a new one. The usual fixes need the original training data or labels on the new network, and both are often unavailable.

How it works

SFUGAG adapts the model using only a frozen task encoder and a small sensitive-attribute head from the source. On the new network it reads only node features and connections. This is research code, under review at AISTATS.

CanonNet

CanonNet on GitHub: Every 3D scan arrives tilted. Can geometry alone stand it upright?

  • No training
  • 21 object types

Python, point clouds, S3DIS

What it is

3D scans come out at whatever angle the scanner was held, so two scans of the same chair cannot be compared directly. Most deep-learning pipelines just train on random rotations and hope for the best.

How it works

CanonNet finds a standard upright pose with a fixed recipe: principal axes, then symmetry detection, then a rule for each of 21 object classes. No network or training is involved, and an interactive browser demo is included.

  • 7-day demand forecast
  • Made-up data, real method

Python, neural networks, PostgreSQL

What it is

Shops in Bangladesh run out of stock during Eid and sit on too much in the monsoon, while one central warehouse has to split limited supply between them.

How it works
