About

I like building systems where a clever idea has to hold up on real data. That has meant GPU code that searches thousands of papers in a fraction of a millisecond, language models whose answers are checked before they are shown, services that talk to each other through Kafka, and molecule energies computed on quantum hardware.

I'm drawn to problems that have a right answer to check against: a schedule that must obey every constraint, a quote that must exist in the paper, a simulation that must match the exact result.

AI

Science claim checker

Type a science claim, like "bariatric surgery helps mental health". The program finds the most relevant research papers, an AI reads them, and it answers supported, contradicted or not enough info. It explains why with quotes, and every quote is automatically checked against the paper. Searching 5,183 abstracts takes under a tenth of a millisecond because the search runs on the GPU.

  • Llama 3.2

GridWise

A campus has solar panels, a battery and the power grid. The operator leaves notes like "clean the solar panels from noon to 2 PM". GridWise works out which notes change the plan, builds the cheapest power schedule for the day, and double-checks it. It runs live on Azure.

Molecules on a quantum computer

A molecule's energy changes as its atoms stretch or bend. This project estimates that energy for eleven molecules with a quantum method, checks every answer against the exact one, and rebuilds whole curves from a few points. One run used a real IBM quantum computer.

Science

SACA-Tox

Is this molecule toxic? The model looks at it two ways, as a network of atoms and bonds and as a line of text that describes it, and combines both views to decide. On six standard toxicity tests its AUROC score, where 1.0 is perfect, ranges from 0.84 to 0.99.
